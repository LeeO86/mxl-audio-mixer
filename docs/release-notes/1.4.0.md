# mxl-audio-mixer 1.4.0 — release notes

The operators' feedback on the console, DSP and formats, and the views, and two
fixes to how inputs are read, from the test-all deployment of 1.3.0 (D-263).
Released 2026-10-22 (tag `v1.4.0`, `b34fe4a`).

## Upgrade and rollback — read first

- **Export the show before upgrading:** `GET /api/v1/config/export` (or keep a copy
  of `/config/mix.json`). An upgrade from 1.3.0 is in place (the same `/config` and
  output domain). The 1.3.0 `mix.json`, the show file, the inputs and the IS-05
  connections load unchanged.
- **Rollback to 1.3.0 loses the mix once 1.4.0 features are used.** A show with a
  **subgroup** bus (also one made by the `group` operation) or a **changed
  down-mix** (the mix's, a bus's or a monitor's) cannot be read by 1.3.0
  (`unknown field subgroup` / `downmix`). 1.3.0 then does **not** refuse to start:
  it renames the file to `/config/mix.json.bad` and **starts with an empty mix**.
  To roll back, clear the subgroup flags and return every down-mix to the mix's
  default first, or restore the show exported before the upgrade.
- A 1.4.0 show without those (the mix's down-mix left at the default is not
  written to `mix.json`) loads in 1.3.0 and sounds the same (measured, below).
- Nodes new in 1.4.0 (`width`, `surround-pan`) and EQ `high-pass`/`low-pass` bands
  are not known to 1.3.0 either; expect a show that uses them to fail there in the
  same way (not tested).

## For the platform

- **New setting `MIXER_INPUT_DELAY_HOLD_S`** (default 30, 0–3600 s): an
  automatic input delay goes down only after the measured need stayed at least
  1 ms lower for this long, and at most once per this time; it goes up at once.
  The default needs no change. Every other setting is as in 1.3.0.
- **`MIXER_READ_OFFSET` can go back to the default 2.** The 20 ms workaround on
  test-all was needed because inputs whose writers commit in batches (the ST 2110
  gateway's bursts of packets, the browser source's audio per video frame) lost
  samples at 2 ms; 1.4.0 measures what those inputs need and gives each the
  automatic delay it needs (about 19 ms for a browser source, a few ms for gateway
  bursts). A larger read offset adds that latency to every input.
- **The input lag metrics change meaning** (same names): `input_arrival_lag_ms`
  (p50, p99, p99.9) and `input_lag_ms` are now the end of the block read minus
  the flow's head index, measured in **every** block (before: TAI now minus the
  head, only in blocks right after a commit). A block is late exactly when its lag
  is above the read offset plus the input's delay. Expect larger values for
  batched writers: about **20 ms** for a browser source (960-sample commits), the
  burst length for gateway audio; dashboards or alerts with thresholds on these
  need a look.
- **No metric is renamed, added or removed.** `input_effective_delay_ms` changes
  much less often (hold time).
- **API additions** (all backwards compatible):
  - `downmix` (`{"centre_db", "surround_db", "lfe_db", "normalise"}`) in
    `/api/v1/mix/settings`, and on buses and monitors (`null`: the mix's);
  - `subgroup` on buses; the `group` operation makes subgroups;
  - `PATCH` `format` on strips and buses (answer `warnings`), `chain_warnings`
    on `GET` of strips and buses;
  - nodes `width` and `surround-pan`, EQ bands `high-pass` and `low-pass`
    (`slope_db_per_oct`);
  - `mute_channels` and `solo_channels` on monitors;
  - `follows_solo` on personal monitors (`/api/v1/personal`);
  - `GET /api/v1/taps` (the taps each strip and bus offers);
  - strips can be published (`PUT /api/v1/outputs/<strip>`: its direct out,
    post-fader before the pan; it was refused);
  - `DELETE` of a strip or bus no longer refuses when other things refer to it: it
    clears those references (logic blocks stay with an empty target).
- Ports, volumes, CPUs, real time, preview and identity: unchanged from 1.3.0
  (see `docs/release-notes/1.3.0.md`, "What the platform must set" and
  "Catalog facts").

## Changes

### Console (#40, #41)

- Rename a strip in place by clicking its name; a gear opens its processing (C1).
- A draggable divider between the strips and the buses/VCAs; each side scrolls on
  its own (C2).
- "Link" in the input patch of stereo and wider strips: pick the first channel,
  the others follow (C3).
- A new strip or bus is selected at once (C4); "+ Strip" and "+ Bus" add several
  at once with numbered names (C6).
- One polarity button on wider strips with a toggle per channel (C5).
- Auto-mixes in their own dialog, members as tiles of one size (C7).
- Strip menu: "Send to…", on a bus "Add source…", "Delete…" with a confirmation
  that lists what it removes, and "Format…" (C9, C10, C11).
- **Subgroup buses** (`subgroup: true`): every send into one is taken from its
  source's output at 0 dB, locked; the bus fader is the group level (C12).

### DSP and formats (#38)

- **Format change** of a strip or bus in operation (C10, P3): the chain stays,
  nodes adapt or are bypassed and flagged (`chain_warnings`), sends use the width
  mapping, the patch is trimmed or extended; the change fades.
- **Adjustable down-mix** (D1/D2): centre, surround and LFE levels and
  normalisation, for the mix, with an override per bus and per monitor. The
  default is 1.3's fixed down-mix.
- New node **`width`** (M/S, width 0–200 %) (D3).
- EQ bands **`high-pass`** and **`low-pass`** with 12–48 dB/oct (D4).
- New node **`surround-pan`** for mono and stereo strips: 5.1 or 7.1, position,
  spread, divergence, LFE (D5).
- Ramping gain matrices cost about a third of before.

### Views (#39)

- Control room: Cut is called Mute (R1); mute and solo of single monitor channels
  (R2); Remove on every monitor (R3); oscillator presets named waveform first (R4);
  personal monitors (My headphones) can follow the control room's PFL/AFL (R5).
- Processing: compact tiles in a grid (P1, P5); previous/next strip with ◀ ▶ and
  `[` `]` (P2); double-click resets an EQ band (P4); the palette stays in view (P6);
  the wheel scrolls while dragging (P7); a click on a tile's header opens or closes
  it (P8).
- Routing: axes named (T1); a new send needs a second click (T2); strips can be
  published (T3); a copy button for flow ids (T4).
- Logic: a click on a block's row selects it in the graph (L1). Sends offer only
  their source's taps (G1).

### Input reading (#37)

- **F1, fixed:** at `MIXER_READ_OFFSET=2`, inputs whose writer commits audio in
  batches lost samples, all counted as late, while the arrival lag read about
  0.5 ms and no automatic delay was set. The lag was measured only in blocks right
  after a commit, when the flow is freshest; a batched writer is a whole batch
  behind just before its next one. The lag is now measured in every read block
  (block end − head index), so the automatic delay covers the batch and the input
  reads clean. A read that MXL answers "too early" after its wait is tried once
  more (MXL wakes readers only per sync batch).
- **F2:** the automatic delay holds: up at once, down only after
  `MIXER_INPUT_DELAY_HOLD_S` of lower need and by at least 1 ms. On the host-03
  fabrics mirrors it changed every 0.1–1 s (7.083 ↔ 9.583 ms), each time with a
  fade.
- `mxl-mixer-tone` writes like the platform's writers (`--batch`, `--late-ms`,
  `--every-s`, `--for-s`); `inputs.sh` covers batched writers and lag outliers.
